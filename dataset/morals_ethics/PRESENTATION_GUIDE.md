# Presentation guide - Morals & Ethics dataset (`morals_ethics_25k.csv`)

Everything you need to explain the dataset to your professor. All numbers are from the final CSV (25,000 rows, 36 columns).

## 1. The 30-second version
A single CSV of **25,000 distinct moral/ethical scenarios** for testing how AI models (and humans) judge right and wrong.
**23,502 rows come from 7 published research datasets**; **1,498 are synthetic** scenarios written by Claude to cover applied ethics
(medicine, AI, business, law...) that published datasets barely cover. Every row says where it came from, what the original label was,
how many people labeled it (when the source tells us), and under what license. It also keeps the 416 items from your earlier workbook.

## 2. Why it exists / what question it serves
Your project (see `PROJECT_STATUS.md`) looks for areas where humans outperform AI. This file is the **Morals & Ethics test pool**:
a large, documented set of items you can put in front of models and compare with human judgments. It is a *pool of items*, not a finding.
By itself it does **not** prove a human advantage: most items record what a crowd said, not a matched human-vs-AI experiment.

**Where files were downloaded from:** GitHub repos (ETHICS mirror `psalm-alignment`, `daily_dilemmas`, `moralchoice`, `MoralCoT`, `BIG-bench`) and the authors' own
Google Storage downloads (Scruples, Social Chemistry). Hugging Face, Zenodo and arXiv were blocked in the build sandbox. Commits and file hashes are pinned in `work/fetch_manifest.json`.
**Caveat to say out loud:** ETHICS came from a GitHub mirror, not the official host; the 400 ETHICS items in your workbook were checked against it and match text and label.

## 4. Who wrote the text (`origin` column)
- `human_authored` **20,534**: crowd workers, Reddit users, researchers.
- `author_written` **347**: hand-written by the source authors (MoralExceptQA, BIG-bench, MoralChoice high-ambiguity, PhilPapers questions).
- `llm_generated_published` **2,621**: generated by an LLM *by the source authors* and published by them (DailyDilemmas, MoralChoice low-ambiguity). Not human-written even though sourced.
- `synthetic_this_project` **1,498**: written by Claude in this project; never labeled; **not human-authored or human-validated**.

## 5. What the labels mean (`label_type` / `human_label_status`)
| human_label_status | rows | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| human_labeled | 20,666 | A human judgment: consensus (9,731), single annotator (6,173), vote plurality (4,614), thresholded proportion (132), survey plurality (16) |
| author_designated | 802 | Authors chose the answer (MoralChoice low 687, BIG-bench 115) - **not** a human vote |
| unlabeled | 3,532 | Open dilemmas: DailyDilemmas 1,354 + MoralChoice high 680 + synthetic 1,498 |

**Key message:** a label is "what this crowd/author said", not moral truth. Use `label_agreement` and `label_distribution` to see how contested an item is.

## 6. Every column explained (36)
**Identity and provenance**
- `item_id` - unique ID. Your workbook items keep their original IDs (e.g. `JUST-001`, `PHIL-001`); new ones are prefix + hash (e.g. `SCAN-55433dc1fd`).
- `origin` - who wrote the text (section 4). `is_synthetic` - True only for my 1,498.
- `source_dataset` - full dataset name; `source_component` - sub-part (e.g. `ETHICS-Justice`, `SocialChem101-dearabby`).
- `source_item_id` - the item's position/ID inside the original dataset, so it can be traced back.
- `source_split` - the original dataset's own split (train/dev/test/test_hard); blank when none.
- `preserved_from_prior_workbook` - True for the 416 items from `CM_Ethics_Dataset_1.xlsx`.
- `n_prior_model_responses` - how many Claude responses from your workbook exist for this item (3 for each of the 416; they live in `morals_ethics_25k_prior_model_responses.csv`).

**Content**
- `scenario_text` - the scenario shown to the respondent. `question_text` - what is asked.
- `response_format` - answer type: `binary_yes_no`, `binary_AB`, `multiple_choice_5`/`_4`, `ordinal_5` (very bad...very good), `open_dilemma_choice` (no key), `philosopher_single_choice`.
- `answer_options` - allowed answers, `|`-separated. `task_type` - what skill is tested (e.g. `community_verdict_who_is_wrong`, `pairwise_which_is_less_ethical`).
- `domain` - broad topic bucket (23 values, section 8); `topic_tag` - finer tag (e.g. Social Chemistry area, MoralChoice rule such as "Do not kill", synthetic sub-topic).
- `ethical_framework` - e.g. Justice, Deontology, Virtue ethics, Utilitarianism, Commonsense morality.

**Label information**
- `label` - the answer as presented in this file (an option text or letter). `label_raw` - the original value from the source. `label_transform` - exactly what mapping was applied (e.g. "ETHICS label 1 -> 'Yes'", "options shuffled, label = position of ...").
- `label_type` / `human_label_status` - section 5.
- `label_distribution` - JSON of the vote/option counts the source published (e.g. Scruples `{"AUTHOR":9,"OTHER":123,...}`; blank if not published).
- `n_annotators` - number of annotators, only where the source states it. `label_agreement` - share agreeing (0-1), only from source numbers (for ETHICS it is the *construction minimum*, 0.8, not a per-item value).
- `adversarial_filtered` - True for ETHICS "test_hard" items (filtered to be hard for models); blank for sources without the concept.

**Quality, splits, provenance**
- `split` - our train/dev/test (70/10/20: 17,514 / 2,467 / 5,019), assigned per near-duplicate cluster so near-copies never straddle splits.
- `near_dup_cluster_id`, `near_dup_cluster_size` - set for the 91 items that have a very similar sibling (kept, at most 2 per cluster).
- `text_chars` - scenario length (median 190, max 4,879).
- `extra` - JSON with source-specific annotations preserved (Social Chemistry rule-of-thumb and moral foundations, MoralChoice rule-violation labels, DailyDilemmas value tags, PhilPapers option percentages, synthetic generator info).
- `license`, `citation`, `source_url`, `contamination_risk` - per-row provenance and usage terms. `validation_status` - "passed" for all rows.

## 7. What was done to the data (pipeline, in plain words)
1. **Audit** the repo: only `data/CM_Ethics_Dataset_1.xlsx` was morals/ethics (416 items + 1,248 Claude responses). Kept untouched; its items are preserved.
2. **Download** sources (`fetch_sources.py`), pin versions, never overwrite.
3. **Convert** each source to one schema (`adapters.py`), keeping the original label (`label_raw`) and recording every change (`label_transform`). Answer positions shuffled with a fixed seed so "A" isn't always right.
4. **Fill the gap** with synthetic applied-ethics scenarios (`synthetic.py`), flagged and unlabeled.
5. **Clean** (`build_dataset.py`): check required fields and label validity; remove exact duplicates (1,161 + 113 + 103 + 102 + 435 + 550 + 2 + 108 across sources); drop 601 records whose identical text had conflicting source labels; trim 15 near-duplicates; keep one Social Chemistry item per situation (544 dropped).
6. **Select** to reach 25,000: small sources taken whole, large ones capped by fixed weights and sampled label-balanced.
7. **Verify** independently (`validate_and_report.py`): 0 duplicate IDs, 0 exact duplicates, 0 invalid labels, 0 malformed JSON, 0 near-duplicate pairs across train/dev/test (35 near-duplicate pairs remain, all inside one split). A rebuild gives a byte-identical file.
Rejected during upstream validation: 26 records (6 with broken encoding, 20 too short/long).

## 9. Limitations to state honestly
- Labels are **crowd opinion / author convention**, mostly from US, English-speaking online populations. Not moral truth.
- Social Chemistry judgments were **sampled label-balanced**, so the file does not show natural base rates.
- Some items are **LLM-written by the original authors** (2,621) or **by me** (1,498); they may carry LLM style. The synthetic set has no labels and no human review beyond automated checks and a small manual read.
- **Applied ethics comes only from the synthetic set**; the published sources are everyday ethics. Little non-English or non-Western material.
- **Contamination:** almost all published items are famous benchmarks that models have probably seen in training. Only the synthetic set is new text.
- **Dependence:** several rows can derive from the same Reddit post across Scruples/Social Chemistry; near-duplicate removal is word-based, so semantic duplicates may remain.
- **Licenses:** ETHICS, DailyDilemmas, MoralChoice state MIT/CC BY. Scruples, Social Chemistry and MoralExceptQA state **no data license** in the files retrieved; Reddit-derived text. Check before public release.
- Tests/parameters of the original papers (models, settings) were not read in full because paper sites were blocked; see `papers/PAPERS.md` for links (arXiv IDs unverified).
- 25,000 = distinct, structurally valid records, **not** 25,000 human-validated items.

## 10. Likely questions from a professor
- *Is this evidence humans beat AI?* No. It is the item pool; a human-vs-AI claim needs a matched human baseline. Most useful next step: contested items (low `label_agreement`) and the new synthetic dilemmas, plus a small human study.
- *Why mix human, author and LLM text?* Coverage; each is tagged in `origin`, so analyses can filter.
- *Why not more applied ethics from real sources?* Published datasets focus on everyday ethics; those hosting others (Moral Stories, MIC, Delphi) were unreachable from the sandbox.
- *How do you prevent train/test leakage?* Splits assigned by near-duplicate cluster; re-checked on the final file (0 crossings).
- *Is it reproducible?* Yes: `scripts/run_all.sh`, fixed seed, pinned sources, byte-identical rebuild.
- *What did you change from the sources?* Only the format, and shuffled answer order; every change is recorded in `label_transform`.
